Martin Kessler is a writer and independent researcher working on energy systems, institutional decay, and the mathematics of complex systems. His research notes have circulated among engineers, analysts, and planners for over a decade. Valete, homines is his first book. He lives in California.

The Book

Valete, homines (Latin: "farewell, humans") is a full-ledger audit of the systems that sustain modern civilization: net energy, material depletion, debt, complexity, demography, the human body, and the institutions meant to coordinate a response. In each domain the same formal condition appears: committed outcomes now exceed remaining adaptive capacity, and the lag between commitment and consequence runs longer than the institutions built to respond. Fifteen chapters, each opening on a verified micro-scene and closing with a formal lag statement. The manuscript is complete at roughly 72,000 words and is currently in submission to publishers.

In the lineage of Vaclav Smil's Energy and Civilization and William Catton's Overshoot.
